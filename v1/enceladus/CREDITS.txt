ENCELADUS · CASSINI-INSPIRED SOUTH-POLAR DIVE

Cassini ISS global false-color mosaic (PIA18435), processed by Paul Schenk.
NASA/JPL-Caltech/Space Science Institute/Lunar and Planetary Institute.
https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/color-maps-of-enceladus-2014/

Clear-filter south-polar image and stereo relief use Schenk, P. M. and
McKinnon, W. B. (2024), New global topography of Enceladus: Hypsometry,
basins, spherical harmonics, shell thickness, and true polar wander revisited.
Icarus 408, 115827. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2023.115827
USGS Astrogeology/PDS source image sampling 100 m and DEM sampling 200 m.
Atlas derivatives use a 2048 px south-polar reprojection, a 1536 × 768
height grid in integer metres, and a 4096 × 2048 global color texture.
https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/enceladus-cassini-global-mosaic-100m-schenk
https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/enceladus-cassini-global-dem-200m-schenk

The eight localized dust vent positions and directions follow Spitale, J. N.
and Porco, C. C. (2007), Association of the jets of Enceladus with the warmest
regions on its south-polar fractures, Nature 449, 695–697, as tabulated in
Dong et al. (2015), Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets, Table 2.
https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06217
https://doi.org/10.1002/2014JE004700

Ice density, jet intensity, fine grain distribution and diffuse curtains are
modeled. The camera and lighting are composed, inspired by the Cassini E-21
encounter on October 28, 2015. The sequence is a cinematic interpretation
of Cassini's 49 km pass.
https://science.nasa.gov/missions/cassini/enceladus-flyby-21-e-21-deepest-dive-through-the-plume/
