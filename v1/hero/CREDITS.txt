EARTH AT FIRST LIGHT
Global and Atlantic region daytime albedo: Blue Marble Next Generation,
July 2004, NASA Earth Observatory and Reto Stockli (NASA GSFC).
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/
Global source: 5400 × 2700. Regional source: 21600 × 10800,
cropped and resampled for web. Crop: 148°W–28°W, 6°N–70°N.
Nighttime city lights: Black Marble 2016, NASA Earth Observatory,
Joshua Stevens, using Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Román (NASA GSFC).
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/
Global cloud coverage: NOAA Climate Prediction Center satellite cloud
composite, NASA Goddard Scientific Visualization Studio visualization 4439,
frame 01000, resampled opacity channel. Polar coverage fades to the Atlas's
existing attributed cloud fallback.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4439/
Cloud height, movement, lighting and orbital timing are rendered models.

ABOVE A JOVIAN STORM
Global color map: NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute, Cassini PIA07782.
https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/cassinis-best-maps-of-jupiter-cylindrical-map/
Regional image: Jovian Vortex View (PIA23440), from NASA/JPL-Caltech/
SwRI/MSSS JunoCam imagery. Image processing Kevin M. Gill, CC BY.
https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/jovian-vortex-view/
The regional image was resampled, placed within the modeled Jovian
cloud field, and animated by illustrative wind and depth shaders. Its
placement and animated cloud heights are not measurements.
